# The Email channel — anatomy from the recording

Source: screenshot of the Email screen at ~0:00:48 of the 35:33 recording.
Presenter's own demo, Auburn NH buyer.

## The correction this forces

The sidebar labels this channel **"Cold outreach"**, and I had been building
toward cold email to homeowners. **The actual copy is not that.** The closing ask
reads:

> "If you know anyone in Auburn who might be thinking about putting their house
> on the market, please forward this email to them."

This email goes to **the agent's own sphere** — past clients, neighbors, the
database — and asks them to forward it. The homeowner is reached *second-hand*,
by someone they already know. That is a different play from the letter, which
goes to the homeowner directly.

Which makes sense: a letter to a stranger is normal, an unsolicited email to a
stranger is not. The letter does the cold work; the email does the warm work.

Two consequences:

1. **CAN-SPAM exposure drops sharply.** Mail to your own sphere is a different
   animal from cold email to scraped addresses. The compliant-footer work still
   belongs in the build, but it is no longer the central risk it looked like.
   Worth noting an attendee asked this live in the Q&A — *"Can you send emails to
   people you haven't gotten permission to email?"* — so it is a real question in
   the room, not a hypothetical.
2. **The email's recipient list is not the parcel list.** It needs the agent's
   own contacts, which the recipient list does not contain. The Text channel
   ("SMS to past clients") is the same audience. So the tool has *two* audiences:
   homeowners for the letter, sphere for the email and text.

## Structure of the generated email

Reproduced structurally; the presenter's phone number is omitted.

**Subject:** `Buyer looking in [City, ST] — know anyone?`

**Body:**

1. **Opening, one line.** "I just got off the phone with my clients who are
   looking for a house in [City, ST], anywhere between [$low] and [$high]."
   — establishes the buyer is real and the conversation just happened.
2. **Transition:** "Here's who they are and what they're looking for:"
3. **Buyer nickname**, first names only ("Jimmy and Sandra").
4. **Exactly three bullets**, in this order:
   - readiness / financing — "Pre-approved and ready for a quick close"
   - condition tolerance — "Open to homes that need minor updates"
   - character and motivation — "A serious, motivated couple with a clear
     picture of what they want — and the ability to move fast when the right
     home comes up."
5. **The ask** — forward this to anyone thinking of selling, plus a direct
   phone number.
6. **Sign-off:** "I appreciate your help," then the agent's name.

Notes on the copy:

- Budget is written in **round numbers** — "$800K and $1.2M". Matches the brief's
  own rule; no charm pricing anywhere.
- Short. Six lines of body text and three bullets.
- Asks for a **forward**, not a reply. The call to action is effortless.
- "a serious, motivated couple" — describes the buyers' *motivation*, and
  mentions they are a couple. Our fair housing screen does not flag this, and
  should not: it describes the agent's own clients, not a restriction on who may
  buy. The line stays on the right side as long as it never drifts into family
  composition, age, or who a neighborhood suits.

## How the output is delivered

Buttons on the screen: **Regenerate**, **Edit**, **Copy body**, **Copy all**.

**The tool does not send anything.** It generates text you copy into your own
email client. That is worth replicating exactly — it keeps sending under the
agent's own domain and reputation, and avoids the tool becoming a bulk sender.

Also visible: a **"Go to Campaign Page"** button, implying a campaign view that
ties the channels together — not yet seen.

## Cadence, from the Q&A

An attendee wrote: *"So I should call next week, if I do not hear back from my
letter — TO DO for next week."*

That implies the sequence is **letter first, then a phone follow-up about a week
later** if there is no response. Needs confirming against the audio, but it is
the first concrete timing signal we have.
