# What the transcript settled

Lauren exported the full Otter transcript of the Sep 3 session (57 minutes,
"Mastering Hyper-Local Content Creation w/ AI"). It closes nearly every open
question and is quoted here where the wording matters.

## The four plays, as he frames them

He calls them hidden gems — features his own customers already pay for and miss.

**1 · Magic Buyer's payload is the recipient list, not the letter.**
Download the list, drop it into an AI tool connected to Gmail, send every
recipient an individually written email. His reasoning:

> "A cold email stops becoming a cold email when it's highly relevant and personalised."

**2 · Deal of the Week quietly hands you a second list.**
Find the listing going viral, and the tool also returns every comparable home
near it — owners who would want to know their street just got hot. He calls this
the hot homes strategy.

**2A · The infinite referral script.** Ten people from the sphere, weekly:

> "Hey Eric, hope everything's going well. I just came across this deal and it
> immediately made me think of you. There's a home in Auburn right now that's
> going viral for X. I know you're not looking to make a move, but because you're
> so well connected, I wanted to share this with you. Is this worth sharing with
> someone you might know?"

At ten a week, 150 contacts are covered in fifteen weeks, then the cycle repeats
with "thanks so much for the referral last time — I've got another hot one."

**3 · Cannonball's buy box.** Ten ZIP codes, refreshed daily, checked against
Zillow to catch relists.

## The numbers that change how the tool should work

**The narrowing move is years owned, not price.** His Auburn search returned
**244 homes**; filtering to *owned 10+ years* left **68**. That is where the 68
in the recipient screenshot came from.

**Days on market is the ranking signal for expireds.** Verbatim:

> "Which of these listings is more likely to convert into an appointment? The one
> that's on the market for one day, or the one on the market for 110 days? Clearly
> the one that's on the market for 110 days."

A one-day listing is an agent cancelling and relisting to reset the clock — the
seller never failed. **Implemented:** Cannonball now sorts by days on market,
flags anything under a week as a probable relist, and calls out the 90+ day
group as where to start.

**The stat behind the whole expired strategy:**

> "20% of all sales next year will be from people who failed to sell in previous
> years. 48% of sellers who fail to sell do not relist right away."

- **Cannonball is both.** A designed PDF dossier that gets mailed *and* a

## The one tactic not copied

He recommends sending at **random intervals over four hours** and varying subject
lines specifically to avoid tripping spam filters.

The personalisation half is right and is built. The interval-randomising is
deliverability evasion, and the domain it would burn is Lauren's own. Our email
path stays honest: genuinely personal, sane volume, real opt-outs, accurate
headers. Recorded as a decision, not an oversight.

His CAN-SPAM summary is otherwise accurate and worth keeping: honour opt-outs
immediately, include a real name and business address, never mislead.
