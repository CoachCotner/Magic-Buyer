Voice per `magic_buyer_letter_master.md`. Round numbers. The letter is about
the buyer, not the building. It asks one question and permits "no."

**Merge fields:** `{owner_salutation}` · `{building_name}` · `{address}` ·
`{units}` · `{years_held}`

**Buyer, as given 09/21:** South Bay family, owns and holds buildings here,
1031 exchange, 15–50 units in West Torrance, up to $12M, closes in 45 days,
will take deferred maintenance.

## Why this letter and not the usual one

- **"One agent in the room"** is the listing pitch without saying "list with
  me." The owner reads it as privacy and speed. It also happens to be true: the
  buyer goes direct to the listing agent, so whoever the owner engages handles
  both sides. That is the whole economics of this mailing.
- **Agency, when it lands:** if the owner engages Lauren, that is a listing.
  The buyer is unrepresented or it is dual agency — either way the CA agency
  disclosure (Civ. Code §2079.13–.24) and, if dual, written consent from both,
  at the listing, not at the offer. The letter promises nothing about
  representation and shouldn't.
- **Never a price.** "Any price" is a question, not a number. The number comes
  from the comps conversation, after the owner has said "it depends."

- **"They have a deadline and the money is in hand"** — a 1031 is the one
  buyer story every apartment owner already understands and believes.
- **"A building that needs work is fine"** removes the reason most 1960s
  owners never list: they think they'd have to fix it first.
- **"They keep them"** is the whole pitch. Long-held owners fear the buyer, not
  the sale. Naming a local family that holds answers the fear before it's asked.
- **"Is there any price"** is a different question than "do you want to sell."
  Nobody on this list wants to sell. Some of them have a number.
- **"Several have on your street"** is true — Amie, Anza, Emerald, Garnet and
  Wood all traded 2024–2026 at $4–12M — and it's the reason to call back.
- Nothing about the buyer's name, nationality, religion, or anything else about
  who they are. "A family that owns buildings here" describes a business, which
  is all the letter needs.

## Follow-up

One letter, then one phone call to the entity's number of record thirty days
later if there's no reply. Not a sequence. These owners get a mailbox full of
"we buy apartments" postcards; the point of this one is that it doesn't look
like those.
