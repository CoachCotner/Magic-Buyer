# Two drafts — 23310 Audrey Ave and 1205 11th St

Voice follows `magic_buyer_letter_master.md`: plain, direct, one question, and an
explicit permission to say no. Round numbers throughout.

Guardrails applied to both: no urgency language, no "we buy houses," no cash-offer
framing, no claim or implication that Lauren can stop a foreclosure, no
disparagement of anyone (Article 15), nothing touching a protected class.

**The servicer-contact offer appears only after a listing agreement.** Offering to
deal with someone's lender before you are their agent is the thing Civil Code
§2945 is aimed at. Once listed, with written third-party authorization, no
separate fee, and compensation out of the commission, it is licensed activity and
squarely inside the §2945.1 exemption.

**Taxes being current matters twice.** Nothing senior comes off the top, so the
net-proceeds math is clean. And current taxes alongside a delinquent mortgage is
a specific pattern — someone is still tending the property. People who have given
up stop paying everything.

**Net proceeds, structurally** (Lauren supplies the sale price from her own CMA):

    sale price
      − payoff, $742,646.37 and rising
      − commission
      − escrow, title, transfer tax
      − any repair or concession
    = net to the Wermers

At anything in the range a 2,415 sq ft four-bedroom in 90505 commands, that net
is well into six figures. **That number is the entire reason to make the call.**

**The payoff is not fixed.** $742,646.37 was the figure at initial publication of
the notice. Per-diem interest and trustee fees accrue. Quote it as "about seven
hundred forty-three thousand as of the notice," never as a settled number.

**On postponement — know it, do not promise it.** Servicers do sometimes postpone
a sale when there is a signed purchase contract in hand, because a sale nets them
more than an auction. That is the real mechanism by which twenty-three days
becomes workable. It is entirely the beneficiary's decision. If he asks, the
honest answer is that it happens and it is not hers to grant.


**Correction to the earlier draft.** The lead sheet's $36,164 is the *arrears* on
the notice of default — what it would take to reinstate. It is not the payoff.
The recorded payoff at sale is **$742,646.37**. Both numbers are real and they
answer different questions, and quoting the small one as the amount owed would be

**Cynthia signs too.** She is on the 2019 deed of trust as a joint tenant and is a
co-trustee of the family trust. Any listing needs both signatures, and she is an

**Free rule-out, better than a title pull:** the notice itself gives the

Trustee sales are postponed constantly. Check it the morning you call.

**Approach:** Phone. Twenty-three days is too short for mail to carry it, and a
business line at the property is the cleanest contact position on the list.

**This is a HECM and the notice proves it two ways.** The lender name says
"Liberty Reverse Mortgage," and the transaction history shows the tell: two deeds
of trust recorded the same day, 01/06/2023 — one to PHH for $1,456,200 and a
second to the **Secretary of Housing and Urban Development for $1**. Every FHA
reverse mortgage records that pair. It is a more reliable signal than the
1.5× arithmetic and it should be the primary detector in the app.

**"Maturity date" is the key phrase.** A HECM matures when the borrower dies,
stops occupying the home as a principal residence, or transfers title — not when
someone misses a payment. Two supporting details: on **08/07/2026**, three weeks
before the notice, a deed added **Botica Milan** to the trust vesting. And the
notice's §2923.55 declaration invokes the clause for someone who *does not meet
the definition of a borrower* under §2920.5, which is the language used when the
original borrower has died.

Ljiljana Botica took this loan in January 2023, at 93, to retire a $250,000 Wells
Fargo note. The balance is about $208,000. The family has owned this house since
at least 1992 — the assessed value of $216,458 is a Prop 13 relic.

**Timing:** no sale date may be set until roughly 90 days from 08/28, and the
sale follows the notice of sale by another three weeks. Realistically **no
trustee sale before December**. There is time here, which changes the tone
entirely.

**Value:** run your own CMA. Comparable estimates on the same block come in
around $1.2M–$1.3M, and this is a larger house at 1,710 sq ft, but those are
automated figures and Hermosa is your market, not mine. Whatever the number, it
is multiples of $208,000.

**Write to Milan Botica, not to Ljiljana.** He was added to title three weeks
before the notice, which means he is the person handling it — and if the
maturity event was a death, a letter addressed to her would be the worst
possible thing to arrive.
